Mathematical & Radiative Transfer Core

Governing Equations

All telemetry in Night Atlas is computed from physical radiative transfer and photometric first principles, preserving unit integrity between spaceborne radiance, atmospheric skyglow, and street-level illuminance.

1. Surface Horizontal Illuminance ($E_h$)

$$E_h(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{I_i(\theta_i, \phi_i) \cdot \eta_{\text{dim}}(t)}{r_i^2 + h_i^2} \cos\theta_i, \quad \cos\theta_i = \frac{h_i}{\sqrt{r_i^2 + h_i^2}}$$
Where $I_i$ is luminous intensity ($\text{cd}$), $h_i$ is pole height ($\text{m}$), $r_i$ is horizontal offset ($\text{m}$), and $\eta_{\text{dim}}(t) \in [0.2, 1.0]$ is the nocturnal dimming schedule.

2. Upward Radiance Calibration ($L_{\text{NTL}}$)

$$L_{\text{NTL}} = L_0 \cdot \eta_{\text{dim}}(t) \cdot \left(\frac{\text{UFR} + \rho(1 - \text{UFR})}{\text{UFR}_0 + \rho(1 - \text{UFR}_0)}\right) \cdot S_{\text{Rayleigh}}(\text{CCT})$$
Calibrated against NASA Black Marble $\text{VNP46A2/VNP46A4}$ ($\text{nW}\cdot\text{cm}^{-2}\cdot\text{sr}^{-1}$), separating direct Upward Flux Ratio ($\text{UFR}$) from Lambertian ground albedo $\rho = 0.14$.

3. Zenith Sky Brightness ($\mu_z$)

$$\mu_z = \mu_0 - 2.5 \log_{10}\!\left(1 + \kappa \cdot L_{\text{NTL}}\right) \quad \left[\text{mag}\cdot\text{arcsec}^{-2}\right]$$
Logarithmic transformation from modeled upward radiance to astronomical zenith surface brightness ($\mu_0 \approx 21.95\text{–}22.00\;\text{mag/arcsec}^2$ natural dark sky).

4. BACI Intervention Estimator

$$\Delta_{\text{BACI}} = \left(\bar{Y}_{\text{T,after}} - \bar{Y}_{\text{T,before}}\right) - \left(\bar{Y}_{\text{C,after}} - \bar{Y}_{\text{C,before}}\right)$$
Before-After-Control-Impact estimator isolating municipal retrofit impact on $\text{VNP46A2}$ daily lunar-BRDF-corrected radiance from seasonal and atmospheric variance.

Satellite-Calibrated Urban Skyglow & Municipal Lighting Mitigation: A Radiative Transfer Framework

Author: Epithanes (Lyceum “Second School”, Moscow) Target Venues: JEI · IJSL · MDPI Remote Sensing Data: NASA Black Marble VNP46A2/A4

Abstract

Artificial light at night (ALAN) degrades astronomical sky quality, fragments nocturnal ecosystems, and imposes avoidable municipal electricity expenditure. While spaceborne sensors such as the Suomi NPP / NOAA-20 Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite (VIIRS) Day/Night Band provide global upward radiance ($L_{\text{NTL}}$ in $\text{nW}\cdot\text{cm}^{-2}\cdot\text{sr}^{-1}$), municipal engineers require street-level horizontal illuminance ($E_h$ in $\text{lux}$) and zenith sky brightness ($\mu_z$ in $\text{mag/arcsec}^2$) to evaluate retrofit policies. Here we present Night Atlas, an open radiative-transfer and spatial-planning framework uniting NASA Black Marble VNP46A2/A4 observations with inverse-square streetlamp photometry and Before-After-Control-Impact (BACI) evaluation across five international observatory and ecological buffer corridors.

1. Introduction & Unit Separation

A persistent failure mode in civic lighting tools is the conflation of upward top-of-atmosphere radiance with ground-level illuminance. Night Atlas enforces strict physical separation across $L_{\text{NTL}}$, $E_h$, and $\mu_z$, enabling municipalities to test adaptive midnight dimming ($\eta_{\text{dim}} = 0.50$) and full-cutoff warm-spectrum LED retrofits ($\text{UFR} \le 0.01$, $\text{CCT} = 2700\;\text{K}$) without violating pedestrian safety thresholds.
